Robotics Research

Human-Robot Interaction  ·  PARCS Lab  ·  Northeastern University

My robotics research focuses on Human-Robot Interaction (HRI) — specifically, how the physical and social presentation of robots shapes people's willingness and ability to engage with them in everyday settings.

I'm interested in the nuanced factors that make robots feel approachable or off-putting, trustworthy or threatening, and how those perceptions vary across different people and contexts. This work sits at the intersection of robotics, psychology, and design.

This research is conducted in the PARCS Lab at Northeastern University under the supervision of Professor Zhi Tan.

Research Themes

Social Presentation

How a robot's appearance, movement style, and communicative behaviors influence first impressions and sustained interaction.

Acceptance & Trust

Understanding when and why people accept or resist robotic assistance, and how design choices can build appropriate trust.

Real-World Deployment

Studying robot interactions in naturalistic, uncontrolled environments rather than isolated lab settings.
