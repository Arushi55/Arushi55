Animal-Computer Interaction

ACI Research  ·  INTERACT Animal Lab  ·  Northeastern University

Animal-Computer Interaction (ACI) is a field that asks: who is computing designed for, and who gets left out? My work in this space focuses on designing, building, and evaluating technology systems that genuinely serve non-human animals — not just their human caretakers.

This means centering animal agency and experience in the design process, drawing on ethology and cognitive science to understand how animals perceive and interact with interfaces, and building systems that animals can choose to engage with rather than being forced to use.

This research is conducted in the INTERACT Animal Lab at Northeastern University under the supervision of Professor Rebecca Kleinberger.

Research Themes

Animal-Centered Design

Developing methodologies for including non-human animals as stakeholders in the design process, not just subjects of study.

Cross-Species Interaction

Building and studying interfaces that bridge communication and collaboration between humans and other species.

Welfare & Ethics

Ensuring that ACI systems genuinely benefit animals, with rigorous attention to welfare, consent, and agency.
