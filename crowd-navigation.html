Crowd Navigation

Reinforcement learning for socially-aware autonomous navigation

Python PyTorch Gymnasium Pygame NumPy

A reinforcement learning system for training autonomous agents to navigate through dynamic, crowded environments. The agent must reach its destination efficiently while avoiding collisions, respecting personal space, and minimizing disruption to pedestrians — balancing task success with social compliance.

The project combines a Pygame-based 2D simulator (using the Social Force Model for realistic pedestrian dynamics) with a complete DQN training pipeline. Multiple DQN variants — Prioritized Experience Replay, Double DQN, and Dueling DQN — are implemented alongside a ray-casting sensor and an 86-dimensional observation space.

Built for CS4100 (Artificial Intelligence) at Northeastern University.

Tech Stack

RL: PyTorch, Gymnasium, custom DQN variants

Simulation: Pygame, Social Force Model, A* pathfinding

Sensing: Ray-casting (36 rays, 86-dim observation)

Scenarios: Home, airport, shopping center

Features

Interactive Simulator

A real-time Pygame simulation with keyboard controls and scenario switching. Pedestrians navigate using A* pathfinding with randomized waypoint offsets to prevent synchronized movement, and their dynamics are governed by the Social Force Model — combining self-driving forces, exponential peer repulsion, and wall avoidance.

DQN Training Pipeline

A complete Deep Q-Network training setup with three enhancements implemented on top of the baseline: Prioritized Experience Replay (samples high-TD-error transitions more frequently), Double DQN (decouples action selection from evaluation to reduce overestimation), and Dueling DQN (separate value and advantage streams for more stable learning). Also supports N-step returns and both hard and soft target network updates.

Ray-Casting Sensor

The agent perceives its environment through 36 rays cast at 10° intervals, each encoding distance and object type up to a range of 150 units. Combined with contextual data on the 6 nearest pedestrians (relative position, velocity, distance), this forms an 86-dimensional normalized observation vector.

Social Reward Shaping

The reward function balances task efficiency with social compliance. Goal achievement yields +300; progress toward the goal is shaped continuously. Penalties are applied for personal space intrusion, near-miss encounters, crowd pressure, blocking pedestrian paths, and wall proximity — with a timeout penalty of -120 for exceeding 1,000 steps.

Benchmarking & Evaluation

Trained models are evaluated on success rate, collision count, and pedestrian slowdown — tracking not just whether the robot reaches its goal but how much it disrupts the surrounding crowd. A benchmarking script runs batch tests across varying crowd densities and scenarios.
